# Design System and Theme Library

This document defines the shared quality bar and a large theme library. The goal is coherent craftsmanship with strong variation—not a collection of recolored templates.

## Shared studio quality bar

Reference direction from previous OXE Marketing work:
- Human, premium, modern, trustworthy, creative and professional.
- Clear typography and content hierarchy.
- Generous but purposeful whitespace.
- Soft depth only where it helps; not every element needs a shadow.
- A white/light-blue/blue and navy direction may suit the studio shell, but client demos should each have their own art direction.
- Display headings may use Poppins or a similar expressive sans; body text may use Inter or a similarly readable family. Choose fonts that support Thai correctly; verify Thai glyph coverage and rendering.
- Restrained motion, clean responsive layouts, natural photography and clear calls to action.

## Theme selection rule

Each demo gets:
- **One primary theme** from the library.
- Optionally one secondary influence.
- A unique art-direction brief.
- A tracker entry before coding begins.

Do not combine multiple themes into visual noise. Avoid reusing the same primary theme until the first set of demos is complete. Similar themes can be used later only if layout, typography, palette, imagery and interaction are materially different.

## Theme library

### Editorial and typography-led
1. **Quiet Editorial** — oversized serif headlines, small labels, generous margins, restrained color; for studios, consultants and craft brands.
2. **Architectural Portfolio** — strict grid, precise alignment, large project imagery, fine rules; for interior designers and architects.
3. **Independent Magazine** — asymmetrical columns, image captions, bold editorial typography; for culture, hospitality and creative work.
4. **Modern Swiss** — disciplined grid, sans typography, strong negative space, limited palette; for B2B and professional firms.
5. **Typographic Poster** — expressive type scale, bold crop, short copy; for fashion, events and creative studios.
6. **Bookish Heritage** — literary serif, warm paper tones, subtle ornament; for boutique hotels and specialist professionals.
7. **Gallery White Cube** — images presented like artworks, quiet navigation, minimal framing; for photographers, artists and premium interiors.
8. **Newspaper Utility** — compact, structured information and strong labels; for directories, education and content-heavy services.

### Warm, tactile and human
9. **Warm Minimal** — cream, clay, muted green, tactile imagery, calm layouts; for wellness and interiors.
10. **Natural Craft** — wood, linen, material close-ups, honest process storytelling; for makers and renovation.
11. **Handmade Modern** — subtle hand-drawn details used sparingly, imperfect shapes, real photography; for independent local brands.
12. **Soft Contemporary** — rounded forms without excessive cards, gentle contrast, welcoming copy; for clinics and family services.
13. **Mediterranean Light** — sun-washed neutrals, warm stone, olive and terracotta; for hospitality and lifestyle.
14. **Botanical Calm** — deep green, soft botanical imagery, fine typography; for spas, wellness and organic brands.
15. **Humanist Professional** — readable humanist sans, clear explanations, candid team imagery; for consulting and B2B.
16. **Craft Workshop** — process details, tool/material photography, confident utility; for renovation, tailoring and workshops.

### Premium and luxury
17. **Understated Luxury** — charcoal, bone, elegant serif, meticulous spacing; for jewellery, tailoring and premium services.
18. **Contemporary Boutique Hotel** — immersive room imagery, editorial copy, practical booking details; for hospitality.
19. **Dark Gallery** — near-black surfaces and bright artwork, used only when content supports it; for automotive, photography or nightlife.
20. **Champagne Modern** — warm neutrals and subtle metallic accents, never fake gold gradients; for premium service brands.
21. **Monochrome Atelier** — black/white with one signature accent; for fashion, architects and design studios.

23. **Tailored Heritage** — classic serif, deep colors, textile/detail imagery; for bespoke tailoring and legacy brands.
24. **Modern Prestige** — strong photography, restrained dark/light contrast, crisp CTAs; for premium real estate and automotive.

### Contemporary digital and product-like

26. **Friendly Utility** — approachable color, excellent form UX, straightforward content; for practical services.
27. **Blueprint Modern** — technical annotations, grid lines used sparingly, precise diagrams; for architecture and engineering.

### Conversion and service-led

57. **Directory Clear** — categories and useful filters; for property, education and local discovery.
58. **Property Explorer** — photo-led listing cards, filters and clear enquiry path; for real estate.

## Thai typography note

Never assume a Latin font renders Thai well. Test the chosen font and fall back to a Thai-capable family such as Noto Sans Thai or an appropriate system font. Ensure Thai line-height and wrapping feel intentional. Use language-specific font stacks and avoid excessive letter-spacing on Thai text.
